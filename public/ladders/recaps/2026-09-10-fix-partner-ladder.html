The Dink Society

Fix Partner Ladder Recap

Sep 10, 2026 · South Bay Tennis & Pickleball Center · 16 players · 8 fixed pairs · 8 rounds · 4 courts

The Recap

Dead Level on King Court: Kayo & Yoshi Win the Night in the Last Game

Two pairs walked onto King Court for the final round with nothing between them. Kayo Hayashi & Yoshi K and Annie Kang & Jason Carroll were both 5-2. Both were +18 on point differential. Not close — identical. Eight rounds of pickleball had failed to separate them, so the night did the sensible thing and settled it head to head on 3A. Kayo & Yoshi won it 11-4.

The pair that went 10-0 on the circuit's first Fixed Partner night wasn't here to defend it — Ryan Hom was on vacation, and King Court was open. Kayo & Yoshi came and got it the long way: they opened the night on 3D, the bottom court, and climbed a court a round until they were on 3A by round 4. Two losses knocked them back down to 3B. Then three straight wins carried them all the way back up for the decider. Final line: 6-2, +25, a night DR of 68.7, and the stingiest defense in the field at 5.5 points allowed a game. Bottom of the ladder to the top of it in one night.

Annie Kang & Jason Carroll will want the round-8 tape back. They led or shared the lead almost wire to wire, spent five of eight rounds on King Court (nobody spent more), and went into the last game tied for first. They lost it 4-11, finished 5-3, and then the tiebreaker went to work: point differential dropped them to +11, one behind Jennifer Witkowski & Matt Pasqualetto at +12. Tied for first with one game to play, fourth by the end of it, off the podium completely.

Which is how Jennifer & Matt squeaked onto the podium in third, by a single point of differential, closing with an 11-2 over T & Richard on 3C. Second went to Karen Terada & Devin Carroll, who matched the night's best differential at +25 and scored more than anybody out there (70 points, 8.8 a game) but finished 5-3 because wins come first in the tiebreaker. The one that stings: the only one-point game of the entire night, an 8-7 loss to Dot M. & Guy Chirinian in round 4. Thirty-two games, exactly one decided by a single point, and it cost the pair that finished second a share of first.

Down the ladder, T Petitt & Richard Hak went 3-5 but posted the best differential of the three pairs tied on wins (−4), and they did it bluntly: all three of their wins were 11-2 or better. They never made it to King Court. Dot & Guy had the strangest shape of the night — 2-0 out of the gate, on 3A by round 2, then four straight losses that carried them from King Court all the way down to 3D by the final round. Leslie Lee & Ralph Espedido spent seven of their eight rounds on the bottom two courts.

And then there's Sally Whitty, whose night deserves an asterisk carved in stone. Her partner didn't show. She spent the first five rounds alongside a fill-in who had found out what pickleball was that evening, and lost all five. A new partner arrived for the last three rounds and the pair immediately won two of them, 11-3 and 11-4, climbing off the bottom court for the first time all night and finishing on 3B. The record book says 2-6. The record book is not telling you the whole story.

Receipt of the night, filed for the record: Yoshi K body-bagged Richard Hak in their round-6 meeting on 3C. Kayo & Yoshi took it 7-5. The pickleball tattoo is reportedly still on Rich's arm.

One last thing before the receipts get filed. Kayo Hayashi and Yoshi K are both off to tournaments this weekend. The Society will be watching the results — go bring back some hardware.

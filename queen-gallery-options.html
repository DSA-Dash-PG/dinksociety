All in Palette D — Pearl & Platinum. Hover any photo to see the per-photo download button. Pick a layout (or mix — e.g. editorial for the newest night, uniform for the archive) and I'll build it.

Layout 1

Justified rows

Photos keep their real shape and rows are filled edge to edge — nothing gets cropped, tall and wide shots sit together cleanly. This is how Flickr and Google Photos lay out an album, and it's the most flattering to action shots where the framing matters.

Layout 2

Uniform grid

Every photo cropped to the same square. Tidiest and most predictable — an archive of 15 nights stays visually calm and scans fast. Downside is the crop: a wide court shot loses its edges in the thumbnail (the full frame is still intact in the lightbox).

One decision needed here. A zip has to be built somewhere. Zipping in the browser needs a ~90KB library added to the page and briefly holds every photo in memory — fine for 12 shots, strained at 60. Zipping on the server is cleaner for the visitor but Netlify functions cap the response size, so a big night could fail. My recommendation: browser-side zip with a cap (say 40 photos), and above that it falls back to downloading them in sequence. You'd never notice the difference at your night sizes.

Under the hood

Two things worth deciding now

Store two sizes, not one. A night with 15 photos at full resolution is 40–50 MB — loading that as thumbnails would make the gallery crawl on a phone. I'd have the upload write a small thumb/ alongside the full img/, so the grid loads the small ones and the lightbox and download pull the original. Costs nothing extra and it's the difference between the gallery feeling instant or sluggish.
Who can delete. Organizers uploading to their own night is settled. My suggestion: an organizer can delete photos on her own ladders only, admin can delete anywhere — same shape as how ladder editing already works. Say if you'd rather deletion stay admin-only.

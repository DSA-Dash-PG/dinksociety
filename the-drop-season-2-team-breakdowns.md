## Lead

Scouting reports are what a league writes when it wants everyone to play a little more paranoid, and that is the whole idea. Week One produced thirty-six games and a table that is a mirror image of itself below the top line: +68, then +18, +19, −19, −18, −68. One team is running away. Four teams are separated by two 11–10 games. One team drew the buzzsaw. Season 2 was billed as wide open, and the Desk's position after one Thursday is that the billing was right — with one very loud exception on Courts 5 & 6.

So here is the file on all six. Each one covers how the team actually wins games (not how the pre-season board assumed it would), where the numbers say it bleeds, which player changes the math, and the honest read on the seven weeks ahead. Under every file sits that team's live Breakdown card — the same one you'll now find on every team page: current record and rank, every rostered player's DSR as a bar you can compare across the roster, the women's / men's / mixed game split, and the clutch record — games decided by three points or fewer, which is where this season is already being decided. The copy stays the way we wrote it on Wednesday; the cards keep moving every Thursday night. Tap a team name anywhere in this edition, or the Team page button on its card, and you land on that team's page, where the new News tab collects every Drop that has ever mentioned them. Yes, including the ones you didn't want written down.

A note on the season's first hardware: the SuprDupr Player of the Week award for Week One went to Big Dink Energy twice over. Tanya Deemer took the women's honor at 3–0, 90.8 DSR and 80.9% of the points she was on court for; Eli Henry took the men's at 4–0, 89.5 DSR, 44 points scored and 15 allowed. Both are in the first file below, and both are the reason the first file is longer than the others. Week Two's winners get named in Friday's recap.

## Storyline 1

**Tag:** The file
**Team:** Big Dink Energy
**Headline:** The team to stop has no pair to stop — and that is the problem for everyone else

**Body:**
**The file.** Season 1 bronze medalists, captained by Angel Munar, picked third in pre-season after losing Richard Hak, Jobeth Zapata, Arica Green and Kayo Hayashi to other rosters. One Thursday later they are first by a distance: 4–0 over FOURPLAY, 10–2 in games, 125 points to 57, a +68 that is more than three times the next-best differential in the division. Seven dressed. Fiat Tapaneeyakorn, Erick Li and Marta Loj are still to play, so the ten-deep roster hasn't even been opened.

**How they win.** Mixed doubles, overwhelmingly. BDE went 7–1 in mixed games, 86 points to 33, and the reason is that the three best DSRs in the league — Tanya Deemer 90.8, Eli Henry 89.5, Chandler Hong 88.9 — never play together, they play with everyone. Eli's four wins came with four different partners (Chandler, Angel, Yoshi Nogimura, Christyn Randolph). Chandler's four came with Eli, Tanya, Ivonne Eastepp and Christyn. Seven distinct pairings took the court and all seven won. A team you can scout usually has a pair you scheme against. BDE hasn't shown one, and the Desk's suspicion is that Angel is doing it on purpose. Tanya's line — 33 points scored, 8 allowed, 80.9% of the points she played for — is the best individual rate in the league, and Chandler's 3–0 in mixed (33–10) gives him a 91.8 mixed DSR.

**Where they bleed.** Small, but real, and it is the same spot twice. The women's games split 1–1 (Ivonne and Christyn lost 6–11 in round two) and the one mixed game that got away was Yoshi and Angel's 9–11 — which is why Yoshi carries a 93.6 men's DSR and a 55.9 mixed DSR from the same night. Both losses came in games where none of the big three was on court. That is the scouting note for the other five captains: BDE plays 12 games a night, 24 player-slots, and in Week One Tanya, Eli and Chandler filled 11 of them. The other 13 are where the match gets stolen. Clutch record 1–1 — they haven't been in enough close games to know how they handle them, because they haven't been in close games.

**The X-factor.** Fiat Tapaneeyakorn. She was the top returning woman on the roster by Season 1 DSR (42.7) and hasn't dressed yet. If she slots into the women's game, the one hole in the split closes.

**Scout's verdict.** The hardest schedule in the league starts now — Smash Society this Thursday, then Happy Hour Hitters, Dink or Swim and Bonkerz — and it is also the only way to find out whether +68 was BDE or FOURPLAY. Smash's 4–4 in mixed against Bonkerz is the number that gets measured against BDE's 7–1 on Courts 5 & 6. If BDE holds 6–2 or better in mixed against a real roster, the pre-season board was wrong about them by two places, and the Desk will say so.

**Chips:** Mixed: 7–1 (86–33), Pairings used: 7 — all won, Top-3 DSRs on one bench: 90.8 · 89.5 · 88.9, Next: Smash Society (Courts 5 & 6)

## Storyline 2

**Tag:** The file
**Team:** Dink or Swim
**Headline:** Picked fifth, built for the margins: the sweep that was eight points from a tie

**Body:**
**The file.** What the Dink?! with a new name, Teddie Liu wearing the armband Sheri O'Neil handed him, and a 4–0 over Happy Hour Hitters that reads as the cleanest scoreline in the league and was the least clean match. Eight games won, four lost, 105 points to 87. Six dressed, all six played all four of their games, nobody sat — and six more (Sheri, Guilbert Balmaceda, Lisa McCullough, Ryan Yeung, Amita Parikh, Phil Monahan) are rostered and haven't played a point.

**How they win.** Close games and mixed doubles. Dink or Swim went 3–0 in games decided by three points or fewer; their opponent went 0–3. Two of those were 11–10, both with Yolie on court, and the round-two women's game and the closer were the difference between 4–0 and 2–2. In mixed they went 6–2, and Karen Clancy is the engine: 3–0 in mixed with three different partners, eight points allowed across the three games, a 93.1 mixed DSR and a 67.3 overall that leads the team. Teddie (3–1, 65.3) went 2-for-2 in clutch games; Shaun Tse (3–1, 64.4, +14) swung +9, −9, +6, +8, which is the least consistent line of anyone with a winning record and also the most fun to watch.

**Where they bleed.** The second men's game. Shaun and Long Thai won the first 11–2; Teddie and Long lost the second 4–11, which leaves Teddie with an 86.5 mixed DSR and a 0.5 men's DSR after one night. That is a small sample and a large gap. The women's game also split 1–1 (Karen's only loss, 6–11). Dink or Swim lost three games all night, none of them close — when this team loses a game it gets beaten, and when it's tight they win. Yolie's −1 point differential with three wins is the whole team in one stat line.

**The X-factor.** The bench. Guilbert Balmaceda played 28 games for K'CHN last season and is the most experienced men's-slot option on the roster who hasn't dressed. If the second men's game is the leak, the plug is already on the roster.

**Scout's verdict.** Bonkerz on Courts 7 & 8 this Thursday is the perfect diagnostic — their men went 0–2 in men's games, so Dink or Swim's own men's-slot question gets answered against the team least able to punish it. Then Smash Society, then Big Dink Energy back to back. Three weeks from now we'll know if fifth was an insult or a rounding error. The clutch record says insult.

**Chips:** Clutch: 3–0, Mixed: 6–2, Karen Clancy mixed: 3–0 · 8 pts allowed, Bench unused: 6 players

## Storyline 3

**Tag:** The file
**Team:** Smash Society
**Headline:** The favorite is 3–1, 0–2 in clutch, and still hasn't played its best player

**Body:**
**The file.** Season 1 runner-up, unbeaten last year against everyone except the team that no longer exists, pre-season No. 1 by a distance. Kai Pylkkanen is the captain now. Week One: 3–1 over Bonkerz, 7–5 in games, 106 to 87 — after a 3–3 first round, the only tied round in the league. Eight dressed and all eight played exactly three games, which is the flattest rotation in the division and, the Desk suspects, a captain still auditioning pairs.

**How they win.** The men's slot and Alex Walsh. Smash went 2–0 in men's games (Matthew Pasqualetto and Stan Esperon 11–7, then Alex and Kai 11–1) and that two-game margin was the entire match. Alex Walsh finished 3–0 with an 88.8 DSR, 33 points to 11 and a 76.4% points-won rate — the fourth-best DSR in the league and the only Smash player above 60. The round-two response is the real scouting note: after the 3–3, Alex moved from mixed into the men's game with Kai and won 11–1, then went back to mixed with Annie Kang and won 11–5. When Smash needed to change the shape of a match, they did it in one round. That is what a favorite looks like even in a week it didn't look like a favorite.

**Where they bleed.** Close games and mixed depth. Clutch record 0–2 — both losses in round one, 8–11 and 8–11. Mixed games split 4–4 despite the roster; five players (Annie, Phoebe Borsum, Sally Whitty, Matthew, Kai) went 2–1 and Jennifer Witkowski went 0–3, though the assignment sheet explains a lot of that: Melissa Sanchez was across the net in all three of her games. Annie Kang, who opened Season 1 at 4–0 and 96.4 DSR, opened Season 2 at 2–1 and 52.0. The Desk reads that as Bonkerz being better than last year's Week One opponents rather than Annie being worse, but it's in the file either way.

**The X-factor.** Ryan Hom. Last season's MVP — 24–6, 72.0 DSR, +117 — is on the roster and did not play in Week One, and neither did Jolie Witkowski. Smash won a match against a good team with its best player on the sideline. Every other team's scouting report on Smash has an asterisk until Ryan dresses, and then it has to be rewritten.

**Scout's verdict.** The schedule front-loads the two hardest matches: Big Dink Energy this Thursday on Courts 5 & 6, then Dink or Swim, then the softer back half (FOURPLAY, Happy Hour Hitters). Two weeks from now Smash is either 3–0 and the board was right, or 1–2 and the Desk gets to write the "what happened to the favorite" column it's already outlining. The men's slot is elite. The mixed slot needs one more anchor. The roster has one; he just hasn't shown up yet.

**Chips:** Men's: 2–0 (22–8), Alex Walsh: 3–0 · 88.8 DSR, Clutch: 0–2, Ryan Hom (S1 MVP): DNP

## Storyline 4

**Tag:** The file
**Team:** Bonkerz
**Headline:** Fifteen players, two women on the sheet: Bonkerz lost a match they matched everywhere but one slot

**Body:**
**The file.** Kayo Hayashi left Big Dink Energy to captain the deepest roster in the league — fifteen names, eleven of them new to a Society scoreboard — and drew the pre-season favorite in Week One. 1–3 to Smash Society, 5–7 in games, 87 to 106. Now strip the result and look at the shape: women's games 1–1, mixed games 4–4 (65 points to 69), first round tied 3–3, clutch record 2–0. Bonkerz lost the match on the two men's games — 7–11 and 1–11 — and nowhere else.

**How they win.** Melissa Sanchez in mixed. 4–2, 62.7 DSR, 51 points scored — the most by any player in the league in Week One — and on court for four of the team's five wins with four different partners: Sidney Chan (11–7), Lee Cox (11–8), Roel Boiser (11–2) and Kayo (11–4 in the women's game). Her split says everything: 44.6 DSR in women's, 69.4 in mixed. She is the mixed anchor, and Bonkerz's 2–0 clutch record came from her 11–8 with Lee and Kayo's 11–8 with Emanuel (E) Escamilla. When it's tight, this team has so far taken it. Kayo (2–4, 42 points, second on the team) is a scorer stuck with the hardest schedule on the roster.

**Where they bleed.** Two things, and both are fixable from the bench. First, the men's slot: Sidney and Lee were a combined 2–0 in mixed and 0–4 in men's games, 8 points to 22. Same players, different slot, opposite result. Second, the structural one: Bonkerz dressed six and only two were women. Two women's games plus eight mixed games is twelve woman-slots a night, and Melissa and Kayo played six each — exactly the new six-game cap, with nothing to spare. Every mixed game, every women's game, the same two people. On a roster with Emma Daniels, Alexis Rowley, Madisen Olsen, Angela Carandang, Jennifer Morales and Maile Toy on it.

**The X-factor.** Jennifer Morales. 8–7 with a plus differential for K'CHN last season, the most Season 1 production on the Bonkerz bench, and the third woman who turns "Melissa plays six" into "Melissa plays four and picks her spots." Pascal Hideux, Daniel Hui and Jason Toy are the men's-slot options nobody has scouted because nobody has seen them.

**Scout's verdict.** Dink or Swim on Courts 7 & 8 this Thursday, whose own men split 1–1 and lost their second men's game 4–11 — the right opponent to test a fix against. Then FOURPLAY, Happy Hour Hitters and BDE. The Desk's read: the mixed play is already good enough to tie rounds with anyone in this league. Turn one men's game a night and Bonkerz are a playoff team. Keep dressing six and Melissa Sanchez is going to be very tired by Week Five.

**Chips:** Mixed: 4–4 (65–69), Men's: 0–2 (8–22), Clutch: 2–0, Women dressed: 2 — 6 games each

## Storyline 5

**Tag:** The file
**Team:** Happy Hour Hitters
**Headline:** Two points from a tie: the pre-season No. 2 has one anchor and needs a second

**Body:**
**The file.** The expansion team that raided two locker rooms — Devin Carroll, Dot M., Pam Morioka and Sallie Kane from Smash Society's Season 1 finalists, Richard Hak from Big Dink Energy — captained by Richard, picked second in pre-season, and swept 4–0 by Dink or Swim in Week One. 4–8 in games, 87 to 105. But the file has to start with the two games that decided it: the round-two women's game and the closer both finished 11–10, both to Dink or Swim. Win one and the match is 3–1. Win both and it's a 2–2 tie. The Hitters lost the round, the match and the week by a combined two points.

**How they win.** KC Terada. 3–1, 60.9 DSR, +19, 43 points (second among women in the league), an 89.3 mixed DSR, and the best line by any player on a losing team anywhere in the division. She won the women's game 11–6 with Dot, then mixed games 11–2 with Devin and 11–5 with Richard, and her only loss was the 10–11. The men's slot is also fine: Rob Scow and Kevin Vu won 11–4 in round two after Devin and Richard opened 2–11, and Kevin (1–2, +3) was the only other Hitter to finish in the black, losing his two games 8–11 and 10–11.

**Where they bleed.** The mixed games KC isn't in. The Hitters went 2–0 in mixed with KC on court and 0–6 without her, and she can only fill four of the twelve woman-slots in a night. That is the entire scouting report on this team in one sentence, and every other captain has already read it. Clutch record 0–3 — 8–11, 10–11, 10–11 — against an opponent that went 3–0. Sallie Kane went 0–4 but was on the 10–11; Dot (1–3) was on both the 11–6 win and the other 10–11. Richard Hak went 1–2 at a 24.6 DSR while his former team went 10–2 without him; the Desk reports, the captain decides.

**The X-factor.** Pam Morioka. Rostered, hasn't dressed, and part of the Smash core that reached last season's final. The pre-season case for this team second was precisely that it had a second anchor. It has to find her — or Shaylyn Ho, also yet to play — because the math on eight unanchored woman-slots doesn't change by itself.

**Scout's verdict.** FOURPLAY on Courts 9 & 10 this Thursday, 0–4 against 0–4, is the softest landing on the schedule before Big Dink Energy in Week Three, Bonkerz in Week Four and Smash Society in Week Five. The talent that made this the Desk's No. 2 is still on the roster — Devin Carroll was 13–6 and +65 last season, Richard 18–8 and +80. The margin the Hitters lost by in Week One is the margin they were built to win by. Two points. They just have to find them, and the file says they're in the second women's game.

**Chips:** 11–10 losses: 2, Clutch: 0–3, Mixed with KC / without: 2–0 / 0–6, KC Terada: 3–1 · +19 · 89.3 mixed DSR

## Storyline 6

**Tag:** The file
**Team:** FOURPLAY
**Headline:** The least informative 0–4 in the league — and the two games that actually tell you something

**Body:**
**The file.** The team the pre-season board called Strangers Dink, now FOURPLAY, captained by Carolina Millan, fourteen deep and almost entirely unknown to a Society box score. Week One drew Big Dink Energy, and the numbers were the numbers: 0–4, 2–10 in games, 57 points to 125. The Desk is not going to pretend that was close. The Desk is going to point out that losing 10–2 to a team that outscored the league by 50 points tells you almost nothing about how FOURPLAY matches up with the other four, which is who they play from here.

**How they win.** Crystal, so far. Both games FOURPLAY won had her on court. The first was 11–9 with Lawrence in mixed — the only mixed game Big Dink Energy lost all night, against Yoshi Nogimura and Angel Munar, and the single result responsible for Yoshi's 55.9 mixed DSR. The second was the round-two women's game, 11–6 with Camille Hend over Ivonne Eastepp and Christyn Randolph. Crystal opened 0–11, won two of her next three, and finished 2–2 as the only non-losing record on the team. Lawrence (1–2) was on the 11–9 and also on the 8–11 men's game with K.L. Chok against Eli Henry and Chandler Hong — the closest any pair got to BDE's men. Clutch record 1–1: when the game was tight, FOURPLAY was as likely to take it as anyone. They just didn't get many tight ones.

**Where they bleed.** Volume. Ron Levin and K.L. Chok went 0–3 in the men's and mixed slots, Gustavo went 0–3 and scored four points in three games, Carolina went 0–4 and played every women's and mixed slot she was handed. Mixed games 1–7. The pattern in the wins is the pattern in the losses, inverted: FOURPLAY's two wins came in the two slots where Tanya Deemer, Eli Henry or Chandler Hong wasn't on court. Against a roster that isn't stacked three deep at the top, that opening exists more often. Most rosters aren't.

**The X-factor.** Seven players who haven't touched a paddle in league play yet: Alejandro Alzate, Kevin Tran, Selene (the lone Timog Cali holdover, 21 games last season), Isabella, Leslie Lee (crossed from Big Dink Energy), Miguel and Susan. Half the roster is unscouted, which is either the most dangerous thing about this team or the least, and the Desk has no way of knowing which until Thursday.

**Scout's verdict.** Happy Hour Hitters on Courts 9 & 10 this week — a team whose mixed pairs without KC Terada went 0–6 — is exactly the kind of opponent where Crystal and Lawrence's opening shows up more than once a night. Then Bonkerz, Smash Society and Dink or Swim. Whoever wins on Thursday gets their first match points of the season and a week of feeling like the board underrated them. Crystal and Lawrence have already taken a game from the best team in the league. The file wants to see them take four from a beatable one.

**Chips:** Games won: 2 (Crystal on both), Only mixed game BDE lost: 11–9 (Crystal & Lawrence), Clutch: 1–1, Yet to play: 7 of 14

### LEAD HEADLINE
The favourites hold serve: ZERO ZERO TWO and Smash Society earn a third meeting, and the final is now a trilogy

### DEK (SUB-HEADLINE)
Both top seeds took care of business on semifinal night — one with another clean 4–0, the other with a first-round sweep — setting up the one championship the division has quietly been building toward all season.

### LEAD STORY
Playoff nights are supposed to be where the seeding breaks. This one held it like a vice. The one-seed and the two-seed both advanced, both did it early, and the only real drama was over how emphatically.

ZERO ZERO TWO did the thing they have done all season and did it again with a bracket on the line: they beat K'CHN 4–0, 9 games to 3, 125 points to 80. That is the same 4–0 scoreline they posted on their last three measured Mondays, against three different opponents, and it has stopped looking like a result and started looking like a setting. Three of their players — Shay C, Kaithlyn R and Masaki — walked off a perfect 4–0 on the night. The only blemish was a single game K'CHN's Katrina Nicasio and Patrick stole 11–9 in the first round, the kind of margin that makes a scoreboard operator sit up before going back to sleep.

Smash Society got there by a different road that arrived at the same place. They ended the Big Dink Energy semifinal before the intermission, taking all six first-round games to go up 2–0 on the match, then split the second round to close it out 3–1 — 119 points to 82, 9 games to 3. It was the performance of a two-seed that knows exactly what it is, and it books them the date they have earned twice and lost twice.

> Eight–nil across two Mondays, and now a third: Smash Society keep booking the same appointment with the same immovable object, and they still have never taken a round off it.

Because that is the final. ZERO ZERO TWO versus Smash Society, for the third time this Circuit, this time for the title. The first two meetings both finished 4–0 the top seed's way — 8–0 on match points, and by the regular-season ledger, 18–6 on games. Smash have been the only team all year that looked like it might catch them. Next Monday they get one more swing, with a trophy sitting on the other side of the net. Down in the bracket, K'CHN and Big Dink Energy — the two sides that came up short in the semis — will play for bronze, and What the Dink?! closed their Circuit on a high, taking the consolation match 3–1 over Timog Cal.

### AROUND THE LEAGUE
ZERO ZERO TWO :: Another Monday, another 4–0. They dispatched K'CHN 9–3 on games and 125–80 on points, extending a run of measured nights that all read the same. Shay C, Kaithlyn R and Masaki each went a flawless 4–0. One win from turning a perfect regular season into a perfect Circuit.
Smash Society :: The 6–0 first round was the whole ballgame — they settled the Big Dink Energy semifinal before the break and coasted home 3–1. Captain Ryan Hom (18–4 across the regular season) steered it and Matt P. went a spotless 4–0. Now comes the meeting they've spent all season earning.
Big Dink Energy :: The 1–3 line is harsher than the night felt. The 3-seed only cracked once the first round got away, and Fiat Tapaneeyakorn battled Smash's best to a 2–2 draw. They finished a single match point outside the two-seed all year, and now the bronze match is a live shot at third place to show for a strong Circuit.
K'CHN :: They ran into the buzzsaw and refused to go quietly. Captain Patrick (11–7 in the regular season, a top-eight mixed rating) went 3–1, and Katrina Nicasio pried loose the only game ZERO ZERO TWO dropped all night. They walk straight into a bronze match they are more than capable of winning.
What the Dink?! :: The night's other winners. The 5-seed took the consolation 3–1, 116–88 on points, powered by out-of-character 3–1 nights from Ryan Yeung and Teddie Liu, both a long way north of their season baselines. A genuinely good way to shut the book.
Timog Cal :: A bruising first Circuit on the results sheet, but Sandee Petersen's crew answered the bell every single week — twelve straight nights, a full roster fielded, never a court left empty. The consolation stayed tight early before Dink pulled clear. Season 2 is a clean slate, and they've already proved they'll show up for it.

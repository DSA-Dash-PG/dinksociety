**Week #:** 0

**Kicker:** The Drop · Pre-Season

**Headline:** The champions left the building. Everybody else came back for the trophy they never touched.

**Dek:** ZERO ZERO TWO went 14–0–2, took the hardware and walked out the door — and the five teams they beat have been quietly rebuilt into six, with Smash Society suddenly the team to catch.

**Byline:** By The Society Desk

**Lead story:**

Here is the strangest sentence the Society Desk has ever typed: the best team in the history of this league is not in this league. ZERO ZERO TWO closed Season 1 at 14–0–2, 30 of 32 match points, plus 399 on the season, and swept the final 4–0. Then they did the one thing nobody else could do to them — they disappeared. Not a single ZERO ZERO TWO name appears on a Season 2 roster. Shay C's 82.0 DSR, Kaithlyn R's 23–5, Lara P's plus 122, Kyle U's plus 130 — all of it, gone. The crown is sitting on an empty chair.

Which changes everything for the two teams that did come back. Smash Society lost six matches last season and every one of them was to the team that just left. Against the rest of the division they were unbeaten. Ryan Hom, the Season 1 male MVP at 24–6 and plus 117, is still on the roster; Phoebe Borsum (15–7, 58.9), Annie Kang (18–12, plus 52) and Kai Pylkkanen (14–8, plus 51) are all back, and Kai has the captain's armband now. Big Dink Energy, last season's bronze medalists, return seven of their regulars including Richard Hak (18–8, plus 80) and Eli Henry (16–10, plus 58) — but they said goodbye to Kayo Hayashi, who is now running her own team, and their two hottest women on the DSR board, Jobeth Zapata (73.2) and Arica Green (67.0), who are not back.

> Four teams left the league. Four teams joined it. Look closer and it is mostly the same people wearing different shirts.

Because the real story of the off-season is not who left — it is where they went. What the Dink?! didn't so much fold as rename: Teddie Liu, Sheri O'Neil, Elaine Dodson, Ryan Yeung and Amita Parikh all resurface on Dink or Swim, and they brought K'CHN's Guilbert Balmaceda and Smash Society's Antonette Petitt with them. Devin Carroll took Dot M., Pam Morioka and Sallie Kane out of the Smash Society locker room and started Happy Hour Hitters. Kayo Hayashi built Bonkerz fifteen deep and poached Emanuel Escamilla from What the Dink?! and Jennifer Morales from K'CHN. Timog Cali sent exactly one emissary — Selene — to a brand-new team called Strangers Dink, which is the only roster in the league we know almost nothing about. Fitting name. New venue, new night, new division (3.5+ Mixed at South End, Thursdays at 6), and a title that, for the first time, nobody already owns.

**Around the League:**

Smash Society :: The favorites, and it is not close on paper. Ryan Hom (24–6, plus 117, DSR 72.0) is the reigning male MVP and the best returning player in the league by a distance. Phoebe Borsum (15–7, 58.9) is the top returning woman now that ZERO ZERO TWO is gone, Annie Kang went 18–12 with a plus 52, and Kai Pylkkanen (14–8, plus 51) steps up as captain. Sally Whitty is back for another run, Matthew Pasqualetto rounds out the returning core, and three new names — Jennifer Witkowski, Jolie Witkowski and Alex Walsh — give a nine-player roster fresh legs. Their only Season 1 problem moved out of state. Well, out of the league.

Big Dink Energy :: The bronze medalists ran it back with seven regulars and a new captain in Angel Munar. Richard Hak (18–8, plus 80, DSR 61.8) is the engine, Eli Henry (16–10, plus 58) is the second gear, and Chandler Hong finished Season 1 in the black at plus 5. Fiat Tapaneeyakorn (11–13), Yoshi Nogimura and Tanya Deemer are all back, and Christyn Randolph is the one new face. The question is the women's side: Jobeth Zapata, Arica Green and Kayo Hayashi are all elsewhere, and somebody has to fill that scoreboard.

Happy Hour Hitters :: The Smash Society spin-off. Captain Devin Carroll went 13–6 with a plus 65 and a 57.5 DSR last season, which would have made him the best player on three of last year's six teams. Dot M. (6–5) came with him, along with Pam Morioka and Sallie Kane, and Leslie Lee crossed over from Big Dink Energy. Kevin Vu and KC Terada are the new additions. Eight players, one proven closer, and a team name that is a lifestyle.

Bonkerz :: Fifteen players. Fifteen. Kayo Hayashi left Big Dink Energy to captain the deepest roster in the league, and she recruited like it: Jennifer Morales (8–7, plus 3 for K'CHN), Emanuel Escamilla (8–8 for What the Dink?!) and Angela Carandang bring Season 1 experience, and then there are eleven names we have never seen on a Society scoreboard — Pascal Hideux, Roel Boiser, Lee Cox, Sidney Chan, Emma Daniels, Alexis Rowley, Madisen Olsen, Daniel Hui, Jason Toy, Maile Toy and Melissa Sanchez. With a six-game cap per night, depth is a weapon. Nobody has more of it.

Dink or Swim :: What the Dink?! with a new name, a new captain and reinforcements. Teddie Liu takes the armband from Sheri O'Neil, who stays on as a player, and Elaine Dodson (9–13), Ryan Yeung and Amita Parikh all return. The big get is Guilbert Balmaceda from K'CHN, who played 28 games last season and knows what a winning Thursday looks like. Antonette Petitt arrives from Smash Society, and K.L. Chok, Karen Clancy, Yolie, Shaun Tse, Long Thai, Lisa McCullough and Phil Monahan make it fourteen deep — second only to Bonkerz. Last season's fifth-place team has more Season 1 reps than anyone but the two returning sides.

Strangers Dink :: Five players, four of whom have never appeared in a Society box score: captain Ron Levin, Carolina Millan, Alejandro Alzate and Kevin Tran. The fifth is Selene, the lone Timog Cali holdover, who played 21 games last season and is the only person on this roster who knows where South End keeps the water. We have no numbers, no history and no idea. That makes them the most dangerous team in the league until proven otherwise, and also the least. Long shots become folklore.
